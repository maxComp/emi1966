The Year 1966

TIME Magazine's Man of the Year for 1966 was not a single person, but rather a generation: "The Inheritor" (or Twenty-Five and Under), representing the Baby Boom generation. The choice highlighted the young, influential, and rebellious demographic of the 1960s who were, at the time, transforming social, cultural, and political landscapes.

Who were they? People 25 and under, born into a post-WWII world. Significance: This generation represented nearly half the US population, shaped the 1960s counterculture, and made up a massive portion of the soldiers drafted into the Vietnam War.
Focused on how this new generation was setting new trends in music, fashion, and social thought.
