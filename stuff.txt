1966 was a pivotal year for technology, bridging the gap between early, room-sized computers and the dawn of modern computing, space exploration, and consumer electronics. Key advancements included the first steps toward home video games, the development of early AI, and major progress in space, with the Soviet Union and the U.S. both achieving historic moon landings. 

Here are the top tech happenings in 1966:
Computing & Artificial Intelligence
Birth of ELIZA (First AI Therapist): Joseph Weizenbaum at MIT created ELIZA, one of the first programs capable of attempting the Turing test, acting as a psychotherapist and introducing artificial intelligence to the general public.
Invention of DRAM: IBM researcher Robert H. Dennard invented Dynamic Random-Access Memory (DRAM) cells, a technology that revolutionized memory density and remains in use today.
First Personal Computer Club: Stephen Gray established the Amateur Computer Society, marking an early milestone for hobbyist computing.
RCA Spectra Series: RCA announced its Spectra series, the first large commercial computers to utilize integrated circuits, competing with IBM by running their software.
HP's First Computer: Hewlett-Packard released the HP 2116A, their first computer, designed as a versatile controller for laboratory instruments. 
Gaming & Consumer Tech
Invention of Video Games (The Brown Box): Ralph Baer at Sanders Associates began designing the "Brown Box," a device that used a standard TV to play games, including a table tennis game that became the basis for Atari's Pong.
First ATM: Scottish inventor James Goodfellow obtained a UK patent for an Automated Teller Machine (ATM) that used a plastic card and personal identification number (PIN).
Home Security: Marie Van Brittan Brown invented the first home security system, which included TV surveillance.
Miniaturized Televisions: Television technology became increasingly portable and miniaturized.

Space Exploration
First Moon Landing (Luna 9): The Soviet Union’s unmanned Luna 9 spacecraft made the first controlled rocket-assisted soft landing on the Moon on February 3.
First Space Docking (Gemini 8): NASA astronauts Neil Armstrong and David Scott achieved the first docking in space with an Agena target vehicle.
Lunar Orbit: The Soviet Union (Luna 10) and the United States (Lunar Orbiter 1) both achieved the first successful orbits around the Moon.
First Venus Landing: The Soviet Venera 3 probe crashed on Venus on March 1, becoming the first spacecraft to land on another planet's surface.

Networking & Telecommunications
Birth of ARPANET: Robert Taylor joined ARPA and brought in Larry Roberts to develop the ARPANET, the precursor to the modern internet.
Optical Fiber Communication: Charles K. Kao and George Hockham presented the first proposals for fiber optic communication.
Carterfone Decision: The FCC supported the use of the Carterfone, a device that connected mobile radios to the telephone network, helping to break the telephone company monopoly on equipment. 
Other Tech Milestones
DNA Deciphered: MIT biochemist Har Khorana finished deciphering the DNA code, a massive leap for genetic engineering.
Artificial Heart: An artificial heart was installed in a patient at a Houston, Texas hospital on April 21.
BCPL Programming Language: The Basic Combined Programming Language (BCPL) was created.
