Collegians learn demanding world of Net service
Idaho Statesman February 3, 1999

A group of Boise State University students are learning that Internet service businesses are demanding in an unusual way.

In the College of Business computer lab students are at work on what industry analysts say is the only student run Internet service provider in the country.

The system offers service to about 60 users in the Treasure Valley. It costs the user $70 a year for Internet access.

It's up and running 24 hours a day, 365 days per year. The students rotate on call duty amoung themselves on a weekly basis.

Having real customers is a key factor to the success of BSU.net. The reality of being accountable to paying customers, even when there are just a few dozen of them, keeps students on their toes. "They pay and they complain".

Learning to communicate with the typical computer user having connection difficulties is something that can't be learned theoretically. It's an aquired skill and usually takes a bit of experience.
